# BlockSize can publish on Edge now, but the latency win needs an internal tap

**Bottom line:** BlockSize Capital can become one of the first external DoubleZero Edge publishers within weeks, because every piece of tooling a publisher needs is public and Apache-2.0 (`edge-feed-spec`, `edge-multicast-ref` v0.6.0, `dz-conformance`, Edge Connect) and DoubleZero's CTO has offered to build the demo himself; but "Edge for Builders" is not yet a launched program (no public page exists, the on-chain `allow-staked-feeds` flag was "on no cluster" as of 11 September 2026 and a DoubleZero engineer wrote on 30 September that "no builder payout exists yet"), so every commercial term from the 16 October call is a bespoke, pre-launch promise that must be put in writing ([PR #4383](https://github.com/malbeclabs/doublezero/pull/4383); [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/malbeclabs/doublezero/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)). The most important technical finding is that BlockSize's "~250 ms" is not a wire measurement at all: it is the documented **250 millisecond batching interval** of the Matrix WebSocket API, and a demo publisher that consumes that WebSocket (as agreed on the call) inherits the batch delay in full, so it changes the delivery mechanism, not the freshness ([Matrix API docs](https://matrix.blocksize.capital/api-docs.json)). Real latency gains come from three places, in order of value: tapping BlockSize's aggregation engine before the batch timer (removes 0–250 ms), ingesting venues in-region (Binance in Tokyo at about **4 ms p50** from exchange timestamp) and carrying that data over DoubleZero's fiber, whose Tokyo–Frankfurt path measures **133.9 ms RTT against about 193 ms on the public internet**, roughly 30 ms one-way saved plus microsecond jitter ([Deltix](https://ember.deltixlab.com/docs/performance/ws-market-data/); [DoubleZero links API](https://data.doublezero.xyz/api/dz/links?limit=500); [Hats Network](https://hatsnet.io/docs/network/latency)). Economically the opportunity is small today and token-exposed: comparable Edge feeds list at $50–$1,500 per month, the publisher share is 45% of gross paid in 2Z, and 2Z hit its all-time low of $0.0315 on 15 October 2026 (down about 96% from the October 2025 high) while about 31 million tokens vest every week ([Hyperliquid Edge docs](https://docs.doublezero.xyz/hyperliquid/edge/); [Federa on X](https://x.com/Austin_Federa/status/2103898130202644799); [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/doublezero); [Tokenomist](https://tokenomist.ai/research/weekly-unlock-digest-sep-28-oct-4-2026-2z-faces-a-114m-cliff-worth-47-of-float-2)). The recommendation is therefore to say yes to the demo on DoubleZero's engineering time, to design the production feed around a pre-batch tap from day one, to treat DoubleZero as a transport for BlockSize's own ingest as the most durable value regardless of Edge's economics, and to decline the role of Binance publisher until a written licence or indemnity exists, because exchange terms (OKX, Coinbase, Kraken, Binance) all prohibit redistributing raw streams without consent and the operator carries that exposure, not DoubleZero ([OKX API Agreement](https://www.okx.com/help/okx-api-agreement); [Coinbase Market Data Terms](https://www.coinbase.com/legal/market_data)).

Distribute your market data over DoubleZero" contact block and the quote-priced "Multicast as a Service" page ([DoubleZero Edge page](https://doublezero.xyz/dz-edge); [doublezero.xyz/multicast](https://doublezero.xyz/multicast)). The call's statement that the program was "announced the week before" is therefore best read as an announcement to selected counterparties, consistent with the code chronology but unverified publicly.

## The 250 ms is a batch timer, and the wire is a minority of it

BlockSize's public API documentation says of real-time VWAP and bid/ask: "Updates will be sent in 250 millisecond batches. Updates will immediately be sent when the price of an asset has changed and re-sent if the price has remained unchanged for 60 consecutive seconds"; the documentation's own example update carries a `ts` exactly 250 ms after the snapshot ([Matrix API docs](https://matrix.blocksize.capital/api-docs.json)). No measured end-to-end figure of 250 ms exists in BlockSize's docs or in the Agentic Payments repository; the only third-party echo is an unclaimed Datarade profile claiming "<250ms latency" ([Datarade profile](https://datarade.ai/data-providers/blocksize/profile)). Two further facts shape the budget. First, BlockSize's rows carry no server send timestamp: the VWAP `ts` is "the last timestamp (exchange provided) of the last trade used for calculation", the bid/ask `ts` is a microsecond UTC value of undocumented origin, and `time_now` returns whole seconds, so nobody outside BlockSize can separate exchange lag from aggregation lag from batching ([Matrix API docs](https://matrix.blocksize.capital/api-docs.json)). Second, both `data.blocksize.capital` and `matrix.blocksize.capital` resolve to one Google Cloud anycast address, and a warm-connection round trip from Texas for a trivial unauthenticated request took 146–176 ms, which together with the Frankfurt headquarters points to a European backend behind Google's global load balancer; this is an inference, since the region is undisclosed ([Pyth blog on BLOCKSIZE](https://pyth.network/blog/new-pyth-data-provider-blocksize); research probe of 16 October 2026 in [blocksize_feed_internals.md](/Users/johannfocke/Documents/Antigravity/Agentic%20Payments/research_notes/DoubleZero%20Edge%20publisher%20feasibility/blocksize_feed_internals.md)).

**Measured on 16 October 2026 (added after the research).** A five-minute capture from Austin, Texas against the Matrix WebSocket for BTCUSD, ETHUSD and SOLUSD confirmed the batch timer and showed that the true end-to-end delay is larger than 250 ms, not smaller. Notifications arrive locked to a shared 250 ms clock (3.4 to 3.9 per second, phase concentration 0.67 to 0.69, p below 1e-199). Receipt minus row timestamp, after NTP correction: trades p50 386 ms / p95 648 ms, bid/ask p50 414 ms / p95 609 ms, VWAP p50 566 ms / p95 754 ms; no VWAP update arrived in under 350 ms. The bid/ask `ts` is a shared aggregation-tick stamp, not a per-symbol exchange time, and about half of VWAP rows were re-sends with an old `ts`. A European vantage point and the Railway region still need the same run to split distance from ingestion and aggregation; see `reports/ws_latency_measurement_2026-10-16.md` and `scripts/measure_ws_latency.py`.

Against this, DoubleZero's fabric numbers are known to the microsecond. On 16 October 2026 link telemetry gave direct device-to-device RTTs of **Tokyo–Chicago 120.8 ms** (one 10 Gbps Cumberland/DRW link), **Tokyo–Frankfurt 133.9 ms** (one 10 Gbps InFiber link), Tokyo–Los Angeles 98.7 ms, Tokyo–Singapore 64.4 ms, Frankfurt–London 10.6 ms, Chicago–New York 16.5 ms and London–New York 64.2 ms, with jitter of 16–65 µs and zero loss on all but one link; shortest multi-hop paths computed from the same data give Tokyo–New York about 137.5 ms and Tokyo–London about 144.8 ms RTT ([DoubleZero links API](https://data.doublezero.xyz/api/dz/links?limit=500)). Halved, these are one-way propagation floors of roughly 60 ms Tokyo→Chicago, 67 ms Tokyo→Frankfurt, 69 ms Tokyo→New York and 72 ms Tokyo→London, which is exactly the "fast delivery to Chicago, New York, London, Frankfurt" value the call attributed to the Binance publisher. The public-internet reference RTTs are about 193 ms Tokyo–Frankfurt, 203 ms Tokyo–London and 145 ms Tokyo–New York, so DoubleZero's edge is large on the Asia–Europe corridors (about 30 ms one-way) and marginal on Tokyo–New York; DoubleZero's own figure is an average 21.6% RTT improvement across 66 corridors ([Hats Network](https://hatsnet.io/docs/network/latency); [Q2 2026 Token Holder Update](https://doublezero.xyz/journal/doublezero-q2-2026-token-holder-update)). The call's framing of DoubleZero as "the middle ground between public internet and top HFT networks" fits these numbers: a 1.25× ratio to the theoretical fiber floor is what a well-provisioned private path achieves, and DoubleZero's Tokyo–Frankfurt 134 ms sits near that, while microwave-grade HFT networks are a different category ([HPBN latency primer](https://hpbn.co/primer-on-latency-and-bandwidth/)).

## A BlockSize Edge publisher: architecture, spec mapping and placement

The reference architecture is fixed by DoubleZero's crates, and BlockSize should not design around it. A venue publisher is one Rust type implementing `dz_adapter_core::Adapter` (`message_types`, `poll_listings`, `on_connected`, `poll_upstream`, `on_payload`, `on_disconnected`, `snapshot`), compiled into a binary that is "a registry and nothing else" calling `dz_publisher_runtime::run(...)`, with dependencies pulled by git tag because "A tag in this repository is the release; there is nothing on crates.io" (the 30 `doublezero*` names on crates.io are 0.0.0 placeholders) ([BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-multicast-ref/blob/main/BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md); [crates.io](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates?q=doublezero)). The runtime owns everything the spec already decided: "the Instrument ID, the Source ID, the Channel ID, the sequence numbers, the scaling, the Update Flags byte, the Action, the datagram and its 1,232-byte cap" ([BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-multicast-ref/blob/main/BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md)). Ingress transports available today are `websocket`, `fix` (TLS mandatory) and `poll`; `multicast`, `filetail` and `uds` are named but "have no crate behind them yet", which matters for option A2 because an internal pre-batch tap would need one of those or a private WebSocket ([BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-multicast-ref/blob/main/BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md)). The Agentic Payments repository contains no Rust at all, but it does hold the protocol knowledge the adapter needs: `BlocksizeStreamCache` performs the `authentication_logon` → `*_subscribe` → snapshot-then-updates → reconnect sequence, decodes notification `params` that may arrive as a JSON string, and auto-detects timestamp units; `CEXBookCache` holds the only sequence-gap and crossed-book logic in the codebase ([blocksize_stream_cache.py](/Users/johannfocke/Documents/Antigravity/Agentic%20Payments/src/blocksize_stream_cache.py); [cex_stream_cache.py](/Users/johannfocke/Documents/Antigravity/Agentic%20Payments/src/cex_stream_cache.py)).

Data flows in five hops. The adapter's WebSocket ingress logs on and subscribes to `bidask_subscribe` and `vwap_subscribe` for the chosen tickers (both WebSocket-only methods), receives rows with `agg_bid_price`, `agg_bid_size`, `agg_ask_price`, `agg_ask_size`, `agg_mid_price` and a microsecond `ts` for bid/ask, and `price`, `size`, `volume` and a millisecond `ts` for VWAP ([Matrix API docs](https://matrix.blocksize.capital/api-docs.json)). The lowering layer maps rows to fixed-size messages, the runtime packs them into 1,232-byte datagrams with the 24-byte frame header (magic `0x445A`, schema version, channel ID, per-channel u64 sequence, nanosecond send timestamp, message count, reset count), and sends them on the allocated multicast group and ports via the `doublezero1` GRE interface (MTU 1476) to the nearest DoubleZero Device; the fabric replicates in hardware and subscribers receive clean UDP on their own `doublezero1` or through Edge Connect's JSON WebSocket on `:8081` ([Top-of-Book spec](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-feed-spec/blob/main/top-of-book/spec.md); [edge-multicast-ref README](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-multicast-ref); [doublezero-edge-connect](https://github.com/malbeclabs/doublezero-edge-connect)).

Mapping BlockSize's products onto the spec family is the one design question without a settled answer. BlockSize's aggregated bid/ask maps almost naturally onto the TOB `Quote` (0x03, 60 bytes): bid and ask price as `i64` with a per-instrument implied exponent, quantities as `u64`, a venue `Source Timestamp`, and, usefully, **`Bid Source Count` and `Ask Source Count` u16 fields** that already anticipate a quote built from several sources ([Top-of-Book spec](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-feed-spec/blob/main/top-of-book/spec.md)). The obstacle is the `Source ID`: the registry says "A Source ID names a matching engine, not a venue", assigned IDs are 1 Hyperliquid, 2 Phoenix, 3 Kalshi, 4–5 Setai, 6 Binance USD-M, 7 XYZ, 8 Binance Spot, 9 Binance Coin-M, and new IDs are requested by pull request; a cross-venue composite is not a matching engine, so DoubleZero must either agree to register BlockSize's aggregation engine as a source or extend the registry, and the range 32768–65535 is reserved for "Private / experimental" use during the demo ([Source ID Registry](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-feed-spec/blob/main/sources/spec.md)). The cross-venue VWAP has no native message at all. The right template is the Midpoint feed (magic `0x4D44`, 40 bytes): a `Method` byte (1 BBO mid, 2 micro-price, 3 depth-weighted mid, 4 last-trade fallback, 255 custom "documented out of band"), `Quality Flags` (stale, one-sided, crossed/locked, synthetic) and three timestamps (venue `Book Timestamp`, publisher `Compute Timestamp`, frame `Send Timestamp`) so that "Compute − Book is publisher-side processing lag. Send − Compute is publisher egress lag"; its principle 8 reads "A derived price without provenance is a number without a contract" ([Midpoint spec](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-feed-spec/blob/main/midpoint/spec.md)). The onboarding guide is explicit that a new feed type must be specified in `edge-feed-spec` and merged first, because "a publisher that decided one of these for itself is the defect class this whole design exists to prevent", and that path "costs weeks" ([BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-multicast-ref/blob/main/BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md)). The pragmatic sequence is: demo on TOB `Quote` for aggregated bid/ask with a private Source ID, publish VWAP in the demo as a Midpoint message with `Method = 255` and an out-of-band note, and open the spec PR for a "Composite" sibling (constituent count, venue-set hash, VWAP window, outlier-rejection flag) in parallel.

Reference data, sequencing and recovery are dictated by the two-port model. Each channel is one multicast group on two ports: `mktdata` for Quote, Trade, Heartbeat and EndOfSession and `refdata` for `InstrumentDefinition` (130 bytes, with a 64-character symbol, asset class, price and quantity exponents, tick and lot size), `ManifestSummary` and `StrikeInterval`; a cold-start subscriber "MUST bind both ports", the publisher must retransmit every active definition within a recommended 30-second cycle paced evenly, and emit `ManifestSummary` about once a second so a subscriber knows when it holds the full catalogue (worst-case cold start about 31 s, bandwidth about 35 kbps per 1,000 instruments) ([Reference Data supplement](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-feed-spec/blob/main/reference-data/spec.md)). Sequencing is a u64 per channel plus a reset counter; a reset means subscribers "discard all cached state"; multiple hosts publishing the same channel is the production pattern (Hyperliquid runs "Ten separate sources", Phoenix "Multiple publishers send each feed on the same groups, channel, and ports"), subscribers key state on source IP plus channel ID and "First arrival wins", and the config option `id_allocation = "derived"` makes instrument IDs a CRC-32 of a key so hosts agree without coordination ([Phoenix docs](https://docs.doublezero.xyz/phoenix/); [Uncapped essay](https://doublezero.xyz/journal/hyperliquid-market-data-on-doublezero-edge); [BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-multicast-ref/blob/main/BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md)). There is no retransmission: "Delivery is fire-and-forget UDP. No market data repair", and a lost TOB quote "stay[s] stale until that instrument's best bid or ask changes" ([Binance docs](https://docs.doublezero.xyz/binance/)). For a stateless VWAP/TOB feed that is acceptable provided the publisher emits a snapshot-flagged full refresh of all instruments every few seconds, and BlockSize's documented 60-second re-send on unchanged prices is a built-in liveness signal the adapter should turn into a heartbeat. The call's comparison to Nasdaq ITCH and CME MDP 3.0 is apt in framing (fixed binary, sequenced UDP multicast, separate definition channel) but Edge deliberately omits MoldUDP64's unicast re-request, CME's TCP replay and A/B feed arbitration ([Nasdaq MoldUDP64](https://m.nasdaqtrader.com/content/technicalsupport/specifications/dataproducts/moldudp64.pdf); [OnixS CME MDP3 glossary](https://ref.onixs.biz/cpp-cme-mdp3-handler-guide/glossary.html)).

Bandwidth is a non-issue and host placement is the real decision. All 2,626 live bid/ask instruments at the documented maximum of four updates per second and 60 bytes per quote come to about 5 Mbit/s (my arithmetic from the audit count and the spec), far below the ≤1 Gbps bond tier and the 50–200 Mbps caps on today's Edge groups; Hyperliquid's reference channel runs at "four or five kilobits per second" ([instrument_quality_audit_2026-10-06.md](/Users/johannfocke/Documents/Antigravity/Agentic%20Payments/docs/gtm/instrument_quality_audit_2026-10-06.md); [multicast groups API](https://data.doublezero.xyz/api/dz/multicast-groups); [Uncapped essay](https://doublezero.xyz/journal/hyperliquid-market-data-on-doublezero-edge)). A publisher host needs an x86-64 Linux server with a public IPv4 and no NAT, GRE (IP protocol 47) and BGP on TCP 179 permitted, the `doublezero` apt package, and a DoubleZero ID keypair that becomes the identity posting the bond and owning the feed; AWS works with the ENI source/destination check disabled, bare metal is preferred, and Cherry Servers and Latitude host DoubleZero Devices in their own facilities ([Setup docs](https://docs.malbeclabs.com/setup/); [edge-connect scripts README](https://github.com/malbeclabs/doublezero-edge-connect/blob/main/scripts/README.md); [Cherry Servers](https://www.cherryservers.com/blog/cherry-servers-joins-doublezero-network)). Because a `Feed` is scoped to one metro ("One `feed_key` is one feed in one metro"), selling in Frankfurt, New York and Tokyo appears to need one Feed per metro, each with its own groups and possibly its own bond ([feed.rs](https://github.com/malbeclabs/doublezero/blob/main/smartcontract/programs/doublezero-serviceability/src/state/feed.rs)). For the composite feed the primary metro is **Frankfurt** (10 devices, 546 users, facilities EQX-FR2/FR5/FR8/FR13, TELE-FRA, IM-FRA2, co-located with the inferred BlockSize backend), with a second host on a device from a different contributor for redundancy; for any venue publisher it is **Tokyo** (9 devices, EQX-TY2/TY8/TY9/TY15, ATY-CC1/CC2) ([metros API](https://data.doublezero.xyz/api/dz/metros); [facilities API](https://data.doublezero.xyz/api/dz/facilities)). The redundancy point is not academic: on 12 August 2026 a Teraswitch routing failure took 28.8% of Solana stake delinquent for about 30 minutes and "DZ users on TeraSwitch-hosted devices were disconnected", and the Tokyo–Chicago and Tokyo–Frankfurt direct links are each a single 10 Gbps contribution from one contributor, with fallback paths that are redundant but not latency-equivalent ([Chainflow](https://chainflowsol.substack.com/p/summary-of-solana-validator-discussions-e48); [DoubleZero links API](https://data.doublezero.xyz/api/dz/links?limit=500)).

Conformance and monitoring tooling is complete enough to build CI around. `dz-conformance` "validates one feed — live multicast or capture replay (pcap or pcapng) — against an explicit rule catalog" for TOB, Midpoint, MBO, MBP and Reference Data "and returns a CI-friendly exit code", which is presumably what the attestor will run; `dz-recorder` captures with `AF_PACKET` so that a datagram "the recorder's own socket would have lost to receive-queue overflow is still in the archive"; `dz-publisher-metrics` exposes Prometheus metrics; and Edge Connect stamps "four latency timestamps for end-to-end measurement" on every JSON message ([edge-feed-spec README](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-feed-spec); [edge-multicast-ref README](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-multicast-ref); [doublezero-edge-connect](https://github.com/malbeclabs/doublezero-edge-connect)). The three-timestamp provenance model only means something if the publisher clock is disciplined; the Deltix method (exchange timestamp versus NTP/PTP-disciplined receive time, with an error bound of about 0.3 ms) is the right instrumentation, and BlockSize should add a send timestamp to its own API so that the Compute − Book term becomes measurable ([Deltix](https://ember.deltixlab.com/docs/performance/ws-market-data/)).

## The demo publisher: three ways to set it up after the call

The agreed next step, Andrew McConnell building a demo publisher in a private shared GitHub repository against BlockSize's trial feed and the existing Rust crates, is the cheapest possible entry because the venue-side work is "mostly" in the venue's own repository and the crate boundary is small ([BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-multicast-ref/blob/main/BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md)). The decision BlockSize controls is what "trial feed" means. BlockSize's public plans cap the Developer tier at **5 active feeds, 1 API key, 1 WebSocket connection per user and IP, and 2 per key**, crypto only, no HTTP, no wildcard; Startup allows 50 feeds and 5 connections per key; Business 350 and 20; Enterprise is unlimited with 100 connections per user-IP and adds wildcard subscriptions and non-crypto instruments ([Matrix API docs](https://matrix.blocksize.capital/api-docs.json)). A self-serve Developer trial key is enough for a BTC/ETH/SOL bid/ask demo, but its single WebSocket connection cannot feed two redundant publisher hosts, and Enterprise-scope features (metals, FX) are excluded. The recommendation is a **scoped key on BlockSize's own Enterprise account** with explicit permission for DoubleZero's engineers to use it, which costs BlockSize nothing and removes the connection ceiling.

Three setup variants are worth proposing, in increasing ambition. The first is the minimal demo as agreed: adapter over `bidask_subscribe` for BTCUSD, ETHUSD and SOLUSD (the pairs that were fresh on both VWAP and bid/ask across many CEX venues in the 6 October audit, which found 1,821 symbols fresh on both), publishing TOB `Quote` messages with a private Source ID in the 32768–65535 range, proven offline with the crates' `[adapter.replay]` facility and passed through `dz-conformance` before any network is touched ([instrument_quality_audit_2026-10-06.md](/Users/johannfocke/Documents/Antigravity/Agentic%20Payments/docs/gtm/instrument_quality_audit_2026-10-06.md); [Source ID Registry](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-feed-spec/blob/main/sources/spec.md)). The second adds `vwap_subscribe` published as Midpoint `Method = 255` plus `fixedvwap_subscribe` (daily 24-hour VWAP at 00:00 UTC) as a slow reference channel, which exercises the derived-price provenance model and gives DoubleZero a concrete artefact for the spec discussion. The third runs the demo on DoubleZero testnet from a Frankfurt host (`doublezero connect multicast --publish <group>` once DoubleZero's infrastructure team allocates a group and port set, since "This repository holds no addresses, no hosts and no inventory") with Edge Connect subscribers in New York and Tokyo recording the four timestamps, so that the first measured latency decomposition of a BlockSize price exists by the end of the demo ([Other multicast docs](https://docs.doublezero.xyz/reference/other-multicast/); [BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-multicast-ref/blob/main/BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md)). In every variant the repository should be private but structured as if public (Apache-2.0 headers, golden vectors), because the spec PR for a composite type will need to point at it. The call's expectation from Ogulcan that a publisher would consume BlockSize's own WebSocket client is correct for the demo and wrong for production; that distinction should be written into the repository README on day one.

## Leveraging Edge beyond the demo: four options, unequal in value

Publishing BlockSize's own composite is the natural first product and the one with the clearest fit to DoubleZero's stated audience ("Data Providers and Oracle Networks" are a named target on the Multicast-as-a-Service page, and the spec text says any two-sided book "crypto spot, equities, futures, FX, prediction markets" is a valid instrument) ([doublezero.xyz/multicast](https://doublezero.xyz/multicast); [Top-of-Book spec](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-feed-spec/blob/main/top-of-book/spec.md)). Its value to subscribers is a 110-venue aggregated top-of-book and VWAP with outlier rejection in the same wire format as Binance, Hyperliquid and Kalshi, which is the "one less integration" pitch Federa uses for Edge ([crypto.news, 12 Oct 2026](https://crypto.news/doublezero-federa-says-fast-market-data-private/)). Priced like Hyperliquid TOB at $900–$1,500 a month, ten subscribers would gross $9,000–$15,000 and return about $4,000–$6,750 a month to BlockSize at the 45% share; priced like Binance at $100, the feed would need over a hundred seats for the same result (my arithmetic on the published list prices). Pricing authority is unclear: the `Feed` account carries no price field and every venue feed today is priced by DoubleZero ([feed.rs](https://github.com/malbeclabs/doublezero/blob/main/smartcontract/programs/doublezero-serviceability/src/state/feed.rs)).

Operating third-party venue publishers such as Binance on DoubleZero's behalf is attractive to DoubleZero and dangerous for BlockSize. Andrew McConnell's preference that partners run all publishers so DoubleZero "stays neutral" (call claim) is consistent with DoubleZero's public framing of Edge as read-only open-access transport, but it also shifts sourcing and licensing exposure to the operator ([crypto.news, 12 Oct 2026](https://crypto.news/doublezero-federa-says-fast-market-data-private/)). The Binance announcement describes the feed as "sourced from the exchange's first party published data", ingested in Tokyo, with no Binance quote and no licensing statement; the AWS region "ap-northeast-1" from the call appears nowhere public, only "Tokyo" ([Binance journal](https://doublezero.xyz/journal/doublezero-adds-binance-market-data-to-edge)). Binance's archived Terms of Use list "data feeding or streaming services that make use of any market data of Binance" among commercial uses prohibited without written consent, its Binance Vision dataset terms exclude commercial licensing "strictly", and Binance is hiring a Market Data Lead to own "redistribution terms", so a licence is negotiable but not published ([cryptomarkets.tools](https://cryptomarkets.tools/guides/exchange-api-terms); [Binance Vision Terms](https://data.binance.vision/Binance_Vision-Terms_of_Use.pdf); [Binance Greenhouse posting](https://job-boards.eu.greenhouse.io/binance/jobs/5002349101)). The economics are also opaque: the Binance feed lists at $100 a month per engine and has 17 and 12 subscribers on its two groups, so the publisher share today would be in the low thousands of dollars a month at most, before the cost of redundant Tokyo hosts ([Binance docs](https://docs.doublezero.xyz/binance/); [multicast groups API](https://data.doublezero.xyz/api/dz/multicast-groups)). Austin Federa's point (call claim) that first-party feeds exist "to prove the model" and that the risk is an exchange later publishing a faster feed cuts against the operator too: the moment Binance publishes itself, a third-party Binance publisher is displaced. Take this option only with a written Binance licence or a DoubleZero indemnity, and only for venues BlockSize already has redistribution rights to.

Edge Connect is a distribution channel, not a product BlockSize builds. It is already shipping as an open-source Rust Docker bridge that joins the multicast on the host and re-serves normalised JSON over `ws://host:8081`, installs with one command, onboards by credit card, and refreshes its feed registry every 30 minutes from a registry "assembled by `malbeclabs/infra` from each venue's published fragment" ([doublezero-edge-connect](https://github.com/malbeclabs/doublezero-edge-connect); [doublezero.xyz/hyperliquid](https://doublezero.xyz/hyperliquid)). DoubleZero positions it for "agentic trading systems anywhere", which is exactly the audience of BlockSize's MCP connectors; a BlockSize feed reaches Edge Connect users only once DoubleZero lists it in that private registry, so listing must be a negotiated deliverable, and the JSON shape Edge Connect emits (venue, symbol, instrument definitions on connect, then quotes) is what BlockSize's agentic customers would actually see ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/doublezero-foundation-turns-two-626-solana-stake-sec-no-action-letter)).

The HIP-3 and perp-DEX oracle use case is real but the pitch is quality, not freshness. Hyperliquid's own oracle is a weighted median of Binance, OKX, Bybit, Kraken, KuCoin, Gate, MEXC and Hyperliquid spot mids published "every 3 seconds"; HIP-3 deployers may call `setOracle` at most every 2.5 seconds, are "expected to call every 3 seconds", and "A markPx move is clamped to 1% from the previous markPx" ([Hyperliquid oracle docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/oracle); [HIP-3 deployer actions](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/hip-3-deployer-actions)). Pyth Pro already sells a 1–50 ms `real_time` channel at "Starting at $2,500" a month and a 1-second tier at $500, hosted "primarily in the Tokyo region", so a "fresher than Pyth" composite has little marginal value on-chain; it has value off-chain for the deployer's pusher and for market makers quoting on HIP-3 books ([Pyth plans](https://app.pyth.com/plans); [Pyth Pro FAQ](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/pro/faq)). The incident record points to the real differentiator: on 28 July 2026 a single-share print at the Nextrade daily lower limit became the oracle reference for xyz:SKHYNIX, moving the oracle from $1,131.40 to $954.99, liquidating about 960 longs and $57.4 million of positions, and the post-mortem listed the missing safeguards as "Outlier rejection, Volume or trade-size weighting, Cross-venue sanity check, Bounds test against the prior close" ([Nexus Mutual incident report](https://nexusmutual.io/blog/xyz-skhynix-flash-crash-on-hyperliquid-incident-report)). BlockSize's outlier-rejecting cross-venue VWAP with explicit quality flags is a direct answer to that list, and DoubleZero's Hyperliquid feed already carries HIP-3 RWA perps and trade.xyz as Source ID 7, so the subscribers are on the network; the pitch to Austin Federa's "opening" is "would have rejected the Nextrade print", not "1 ms faster". Pyth's own HIP-3-as-a-Service accepts "custom REST/WebSocket feeds" as sources, which means BlockSize can serve this market through Pyth's pusher today without Edge at all ([Pyth HIP-3 service](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/hip-3-service)).

## Feasibility, complexity and requirements, ranked

Technically the demo is low complexity and the production composite is medium. The crate boundary is small, the adapter trait has seven methods, the ingress WebSocket transport exists, replay testing is built in, and BlockSize's protocol is already reverse-documented in the repository; the effort is dominated by learning the crates and by the spec work for the composite type, which DoubleZero's guide says "costs weeks" ([BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-multicast-ref/blob/main/BRINGING-UP-A-FEED.md)). The hard engineering sits inside BlockSize: an unbatched, event-driven, send-timestamped internal feed for the publisher (option A2) and, later, regional ingest hosts (option C). The ranking of complexity from lowest to highest is: subscribing to DoubleZero's Binance feed as an input (hours); the WebSocket demo publisher (days); a production Frankfurt composite publisher with conformance, metrics, two hosts and the bond flow (weeks); the composite spec PR and registry change (weeks, gated on DoubleZero); regional ingest over DoubleZero transport (months, gated on permissioned IBRL or a quoted private group); and a Tokyo venue publisher with licensing (months, gated on legal).

Requirements, ranked by how binding they are. People: one Rust engineer for the adapter and runtime integration (DoubleZero supplies this for the demo), one Linux network engineer comfortable with GRE, BGP, PIM, firewalls and Prometheus, and one BlockSize backend engineer to build the pre-batch tap and add a send timestamp; no TypeScript or Python publisher SDK exists, so Rust is unavoidable ([edge-multicast-ref README](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-multicast-ref)). Hosting: at least two x86-64 Linux hosts with dedicated public IPv4 and no NAT in Frankfurt (different contributors' devices), later two in Tokyo for any venue feed; colocation in Equinix FR or TY campuses minimises the public last mile, and no cross-connect option exists for users ("No cross-connects. No contract negotiations." is the subscriber pitch), so a physical cross-connect into a DoubleZero Device would mean becoming a contributor, which requires Arista 7280CR3A switches, AMD V80 FPGAs, 4U and about 4 kW per site ([Setup docs](https://docs.malbeclabs.com/setup/); [DoubleZero Edge page](https://doublezero.xyz/dz-edge); [Contributor requirements](https://docs.malbeclabs.com/contributors/requirements/)). Hardware beyond that is trivial: feed bandwidth is single-digit Mbit/s and what matters is UDP receive buffering and GRE decapsulation CPU, hence the optional XDP `gre-decap` program ([edge-multicast-ref README](https://github.com/malbeclabs/edge-multicast-ref)). Bond capital: about $100,000 in 2Z per feed at the lowest tier, 180-day hold, slashable in principle; if DoubleZero posts it for first movers (call claim), the obligation BlockSize should accept is an SLA text, not capital ([tier_parameters.rs](https://github.com/malbeclabs/doublezero/blob/main/solana/programs/builder-stake/src/state/tier_parameters.rs)). Licensing: BlockSize's existing redistribution rights cover an aggregated index sold to oracles; a raw, venue-attributed real-time stream sold to traders is the use case exchange terms prohibit most explicitly, and Coinbase's definition of "Derived Works" includes "indexes, fixings, or other benchmarks", so even the composite is licensable rather than licence-free ([Coinbase Market Data Terms](https://www.coinbase.com/legal/market_data); [OKX API Agreement](https://www.okx.com/help/okx-api-agreement); [Kraken Global Terms](https://www.kraken.com/legal/global-terms)). Kaiko handles this by being a "licensed and authorized redistributor" for 26 exchanges (not Binance or OKX), CoinAPI by refusing raw redistribution entirely ([Kaiko exchange agreements](https://Kaiko.com/exchange-agreements); [CoinAPI](https://www.coinapi.io/blog/coinapi-data-commercial-use-policy)). A clean separation helps: publish exchange-attributed raw TOB only where a licence exists; publish the composite with constituent counts but not constituent prices everywhere.

## Risks, ranked

Platform risk ranks first because every commercial term is pre-launch and discretionary. The builder payout does not exist in code, the feature flag is off, the RFC is private, the Foundation and Malbec Labs are two Cayman entities with "No DAO or on-chain governance body", and together with Jump Crypto they hold 71% of supply; within the first year they set the validator fee to zero and, per secondary reporting, cut the contributor share from 90% to 50%, so a publisher's share is equally changeable ([Blockworks filing](https://blockworks.com/token-transparency/filing/doublezero); [Q2 2026 Token Holder Update](https://doublezero.xyz/journal/doublezero-q2-2026-token-holder-update)). DoubleZero also acts unilaterally: on 9 September 2026 it removed "unauthorized retransmitters" from Edge with no published appeal process ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/doublezero-removes-unauthorized-shred-retransmitters-claims-70-plus-lead-over-all-competitors)). DoubleZero itself is the publisher for Hyperliquid and Kalshi, so a third party competes with, and depends on, the platform owner as operator and rule-setter. Mitigation: written terms, no BlockSize capital in the bond, and value that survives Edge (option C and the internal tap).

Token risk ranks second. Rewards accrue in 2Z bought with subscriber USDC, so USD revenue fixed at subscription time is realised in a token that fell from an all-time high of $0.75–$0.89 in October 2025 to an all-time low of $0.0315 on 15 October 2026, traded $4–5 million a day on 16 October (3–4% of its $167 million market cap), and faces about 31 million tokens of linear vesting every week through 2029 after the 2 October cliff unlock of about 1.66 billion tokens ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/doublezero); [Tokenomist](https://tokenomist.ai/research/weekly-unlock-digest-sep-28-oct-4-2026-2z-faces-a-114m-cliff-worth-47-of-float-2)). A 2Z bond is a balance-sheet asset with impairment risk; 2Z rewards are German business income at EUR market value on receipt with full record-keeping duties under the BMF letter of 6 March 2025, and receiving and selling them should not trigger a MiCA CASP licence, though no BaFin guidance on token-denominated service fees exists ([CMS on the BMF letter](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/update-des-bmf-schreibens-zu-kryptowerten)). The SEC no-action letter of 29 September 2025 covers DoubleZero's programmatic transfers, not a German recipient's position ([Mayer Brown](https://www.freewritings.law/2025/10/division-of-corporation-finance-issues-no-action-letter-to-doublezero-foundation/)). Mitigation: sell rewards on receipt or contract for USDC payout, which the shreds tooling already supports ([Validator Rewards docs](https://docs.doublezero.xyz/solana/edge/rewards/)).

Legal and licensing risk ranks third, and first for the Binance-operator option. OKX's API Agreement (28 July 2026) restricts market data to "personal, non-commercial trading", applies "equally to Market Data accessed through public endpoints" and requires "a separate data licensing agreement" for commercial scale; Coinbase's terms (7 August 2026) bar building "an application intended for end users" and route redistribution through partners; Kraken's (6 October 2026) prohibit "commercially exploit[ing]" its content; Binance's bar data feeds without written consent ([OKX API Agreement](https://www.okx.com/help/okx-api-agreement); [Coinbase Market Data Terms](https://www.coinbase.com/legal/market_data); [Kraken Global Terms](https://www.kraken.com/legal/global-terms); [cryptomarkets.tools](https://cryptomarkets.tools/guides/exchange-api-terms)). Observed enforcement in crypto is IP blocking rather than litigation and no vendor cut-off precedent was found, which cuts both ways. Hyperliquid's Edge docs also say "The data is for internal use only and may not be retransmitted", so BlockSize must not fold DoubleZero's venue feeds into a resold product without permission ([Hyperliquid Edge docs](https://docs.doublezero.xyz/hyperliquid/edge/)).

Operational risk ranks fourth. There is no retransmission and no publisher SLA (the 99.9% target covers Hyperliquid peering only); single-contributor Tokyo links and the August Teraswitch outage show attachment reliability equals the hosting contributor's; BlockSize's lower plans allow one WebSocket connection per user-IP, which blocks redundant publishers unless the key is Enterprise-scoped; and the 250 ms batch means a WebSocket-fed publisher will lose any head-to-head against DoubleZero's own venue feeds ([Hyperliquid peering docs](https://docs.malbeclabs.com/hyperliquid/peering/); [Chainflow](https://chainflowsol.substack.com/p/summary-of-solana-validator-discussions-e48); [Matrix API docs](https://matrix.blocksize.capital/api-docs.json)). Commercial risk ranks fifth: at Binance-like $100 pricing the publisher share is negligible, the Binance feed itself is "available with an existing Edge subscription" and may be bundled, and the venue can always publish itself (Kalshi waived its share for a year to price "on network delivery rather than data licensing", which shows how little pricing power a middle party has when the venue engages directly) ([PR Newswire, Binance](http://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/doublezero-adds-binance-market-data-to-edge-302901849.html); [Kalshi release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/low-latency-kalshi-market-data-feed-launches-on-doublezero-edge-302849868.html)).

## Recommendation

**Decision taken on 16 October 2026.** BlockSize pursues the demo (Phase 0), the production feed of its own aggregated data (Phase 2) and DoubleZero as transport for its own ingest (Phase 3a). It drops Phase 3b and any venue-attributed raw stream: only proprietary, BlockSize-computed data is published, with aggregate provenance but no constituent venue prices, so no exchange licence or DoubleZero indemnity is needed. The scoped workstreams, owners, gates and trimmed question list are in [doublezero_execution_plan_2026-10-16.md](doublezero_execution_plan_2026-10-16.md).

Proceed with the demo immediately and on DoubleZero's engineering time, because it costs BlockSize a scoped API key and a few days of protocol answers, puts BlockSize among the first external builders on a platform whose code is ready even though its program is not, and produces the one artefact BlockSize lacks today: a measured decomposition of where its price latency goes. Design the production feed around a pre-batch internal tap from the first commit, since the WebSocket-fed demo cannot reduce the 250 ms batch and would otherwise be benchmarked against DoubleZero's own 4 ms Tokyo venue feeds and lose. Treat DoubleZero as transport for BlockSize's own regional ingest as the most durable return on this relationship: the roughly 30 ms one-way and microsecond-jitter gain on the Tokyo–Frankfurt leg improves every downstream product, including what BlockSize publishes to Pyth and Chainlink, and it survives any change to Edge's economics or token. Position the Edge feed for HIP-3 and perp-DEX oracle buyers on pricing-engine quality (outlier rejection, size weighting, cross-venue sanity checks, bounds tests, explicit quality flags) rather than on freshness, which the chain cannot consume faster than every 2.5 seconds. Accept the bond only if DoubleZero posts it, and accept the 45% share only in writing with a USDC payout option. Decline the Binance operator role until a licence or indemnity exists; the economics at $100 a month do not justify carrying another company's licensing exposure.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. BlockSize's problem was never the wire; it was a batch timer and a single-site aggregator that is as stale as its farthest venue, and DoubleZero's value to BlockSize is therefore less about publishing on Edge than about forcing the publish path onto event-driven binary UDP and shortening the inbound legs from Asia. Edge's wire-format family also hands BlockSize a vocabulary it did not have: three-timestamp provenance, method bytes and quality flags make an aggregator's methodology legible on every message, which is exactly what the SK Hynix post-mortem said HIP-3 deployers were missing and what Pyth's median-of-publishers model does not expose.

Looking forward, the asymmetry to watch is that DoubleZero wants partners to carry publishing so it can stay neutral, while the venues can displace any middle party the moment they publish themselves; the durable position for a data vendor on this network is the composite nobody else computes, delivered with provenance, and fed by ingest that is itself faster because it rides the same fiber. If the demo's timestamps confirm that the batch window and the Tokyo leg dominate, the business case writes itself from BlockSize's existing customers rather than from Edge seat revenue, and the 2Z exposure becomes a rounding error instead of the thesis.
