# BlockSize Matrix WebSocket latency measurement (2026-10-16)

Generated by `scripts/measure_ws_latency.py`. Latency here is **local receipt time minus the timestamp carried in the row**, with local time corrected by an NTP offset. The row timestamp is the exchange-provided time of the last trade for VWAP and trades, and an undocumented origin for bid/ask. Nothing in the feed says when BlockSize sent the message, so these figures are exchange-to-client totals, not BlockSize-internal or network-only latency.

## Interpretation

*Written by hand after the 2026-10-16 run; `scripts/measure_ws_latency.py` does not regenerate this section.*

**1. The 250 ms figure is a batch timer, and the data shows it clearly.** Each method delivers about 3.4 to 3.9 notifications per second (not exactly 4, because 250 ms windows with no change are skipped). 38 to 46% of gaps fall in 225 to 275 ms and 43 to 48% sit within 25 ms of a multiple of 250 ms, against about 20% expected for an unstructured process. Arrival times are locked to a 250 ms clock (phase concentration R = 0.67 to 0.69, p below 1e-199), and all three methods share the same phase, so bidask, vwap and trade appear to be flushed by one common timer. On the NTP-corrected clock, 90% of notifications arrive in a 112 ms window of the 250 ms cycle (107 to 219 ms).

**2. That timer is not the end-to-end latency.** Measured from Austin, exchange-or-aggregator timestamp to receipt had medians of 386 ms for trades, 414 ms for bid/ask and 566 ms for VWAP. The p95 values were 648, 609 and 754 ms, and the p99 values 1,194, 703 and 1,027 ms. A pure 250 ms batch adds on average 125 ms (uniform 0 to 250 ms), so roughly 260 to 440 ms of the median is something else: exchange to BlockSize ingestion, aggregation, and network transit from Texas. This test cannot separate those parts. The "250 ms latency" claim is therefore correct as the maximum added batching delay and the update cadence, but it is not a measured end-to-end figure, and from the US the typical delay is about 1.5 to 2.3 times that.

**3. The three timestamps mean different things.**
- Trade `timestamp` is per-trade exchange time. Its latency (386 ms median) is the closest figure here to a true exchange-to-client number. Per exchange, p50 ranged from 332 ms (Kraken) to 442 ms (Crypto.com).
- VWAP `ts` is the last trade used. VWAP lags trades by about 180 ms at the median, which fits an aggregation step after the trade. About 47% of VWAP rows (1,191 of 2,556) repeated an earlier `ts`, far more than the documented 60 s re-send would explain (BTCUSD: 728 repeats in 300 s). VWAP rows are evidently re-sent inside batches without a new trade.
- Bid/ask `ts` is not a per-symbol exchange time: in 69% of multi-row bid/ask notifications (735 of 1,072) every row carried the identical `ts`, and rows of different symbols reuse the same value across notifications. It looks like an aggregation-tick stamp shared across instruments. None of the bid/ask timestamps fall on a 250 ms boundary, so it is not the batch-flush stamp either, and it was already 250 to 600 ms old on arrival (freshest row per notification: p5 250 ms, p50 410 ms, p95 598 ms). Treat bid/ask "latency" as age of the aggregation tick, not of the market event.

**4. Tails and symbols.** SOLUSD trades had a p95 of 1.76 s and a maximum of 7.3 s, while BTCUSD and ETHUSD trades stayed under 2.5 s. No negative latencies occurred, so the clock correction did not push any row ahead of real time. VWAP's min latency of 350 ms means no update ever arrived faster than that from Texas.

**5. Limits of this measurement.**
- One 5-minute window (19:45 to 19:50 UTC, 14:45 Central), BTC, ETH and SOL against USD only, from a residential connection in Texas. It tells nothing about busy market opens or other hours.
- Clock uncertainty is about +/-10 ms (SNTP path asymmetry); the offset drifted 0.9 ms during the run.
- A US vantage point adds transit that a Europe-based Edge publisher would not pay. The same script run from a European host (or from the Railway region) would show how much of the 260 to 440 ms remainder is distance. Run it there before quoting a latency number to DoubleZero.
- All three methods were accepted by the plan (no 4003 errors). `time_now` was too coarse (whole seconds) to cross-check the offset beyond a sanity check.
